# Continue learning

Read the sibling [shared project contract](../learn-start/references/project-contract.md). Load the project resume, current section and evidence relevant to its exit criteria. Reuse the learner's budget and source choices.

## Choose the next action

If the learner says “continue”, resume the saved pending task instead of advancing automatically. If they explicitly request the next section, compare the current section's actual evidence with its exit check. Show the result plainly:

- Ready: summarize what was demonstrated and proceed to a prerequisite-ready section.
- Uncertain: offer a small readiness check on the missing outcome.
- Gap: explain the specific prerequisite and propose short remediation.

A learner may knowingly skip a section. Record it as skipped or leave the unfinished section in progress, preserve evidence gaps, and continue to the requested topic with the necessary scaffolding. A skip is not a pass. Respect an already expressed choice; do not require repeated permission. Optional overdue review should not become an arbitrary barrier to every new lesson.

## Prepare and teach

Check the next section's source mapping. Read the relevant part of the learner's chosen book or source if accessible. When material is missing, ask for the needed resource or offer to find a suitable source; avoid pretending to have read unavailable chapters.

Replace the section's outline with actual theory, worked examples, independent practice and exit criteria before marking materials ready. Keep solutions separate from learner tasks. Prepare a small executable lesson, not the entire remaining course at once.

At the start of each new lesson, show its observable competencies and their connection to the goal, then deliver the full theoretical foundation within that lesson's scope under the shared lesson contract. Save it in a dedicated Markdown file and link it in chat. Include explanations, definitions, connections, assumptions, limits, and worked examples with inspected authoritative sources; a task-solving hint is not a substitute for the theory. Split an oversized topic into coherent lessons rather than withholding essential theory until after practice.

After the theoretical block, invite questions and ask whether the learner is ready for practice; wait for their reply unless they already explicitly requested immediate practice after the explanation. Resume an existing pending task without restarting an already delivered lesson. During practice, follow the shared feedback rules: accept valid answers with a short explanation, correct relevant errors, and clarify only consequential ambiguity. Use graduated hints when needed and record actual assistance. Recurring difficulty can change the explanation or reveal a prerequisite; a correct answer does not require another proof of understanding unless a planned task calls for it.

At the stopping point, save the session, update the current section, evidence, review needs and exact next action under the shared contract. Validate the project. Mark coverage completed only when the section has been worked through; evaluate its exit criteria separately and report any remaining gaps.
